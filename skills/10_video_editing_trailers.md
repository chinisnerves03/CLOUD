# Video editing for mod trailers

## Tools
- DaVinci Resolve (free, strong colour + audio), CapCut (fast for shorts), Premiere/After Effects (paid). Pick one and master it.
- Keep proxies/optimised media if the machine lags.

## Trailer structure (30–60 s) 
1. **0–3 s hook**: best visual moment or question ("What if a 90s hatchback had 500 hp?").
2. **3–10 s**: reveal name + core promise.
3. **10–35 s**: escalating montage — drive, drift, jump, then crash; vary shot size.
4. **35–50 s**: feature proof (configs, parts, sound).
5. **Last 5 s**: call to action + name + where to get it, hold on a strong frame.
Longer showcase (3–8 min): hook → overview → feature walkthrough with chapters → test drive → crash test → outro.

## Pacing rules
- Cut on action or on beat; average shot 1.5–3 s in trailers.
- Alternate speed: fast, slow-mo, fast. Use speed ramps (100% → 20% → 100%) into impacts.
- Match motion direction across cuts; avoid jump cuts of the same angle.
- Start each segment on a strong frame; remove dead time.

## Colour & finish
- Normalise exposure, add gentle contrast, keep the car's paint believable.
- Consistent LUT/grade across all your videos = brand look.
- Titles: simple, 1–2 fonts, large, readable on phone, safe margins.
- Add subtle film grain/vignette, optional letterbox for cinematic mood.

## Sound
See `audio_sound_design`: music under -18 dB, engine and impact SFX, voice ducking, final **-14 LUFS**.

## Export
- Master: H.264/H.265 MP4, 1080p or 4K, 60 fps if recorded so, 15–50 Mbps; AAC 320 kbps audio.
- Vertical: separate 9:16 cut (1080×1920) — don't just crop; reframe each shot **[see short-form skill]**.
- Always export a cover/thumbnail frame and an SRT subtitle file.

## Efficiency
- Template project: intro/outro, lower-thirds, LUT, audio chain.
- Keep a bin of reusable b-roll per mod.
- Review on a phone before publishing.

## Lesson: the hook is not the problem, what follows it is
Source: Instagram reel https://www.instagram.com/reel/DeXnbhxurWk/ (@viewcci; caption only was read, the video itself was not watched).
- After the hook, do not explain: delay the answer so the viewer keeps wanting it (open loop, then reveal in steps).
- Cut every second between hook and payoff that does not raise curiosity; the second 3-8 is where viewers leave.
- End right after the payoff. No long outro, no recap.
- Check the retention graph after posting: find the drop, re-cut that spot next time.
- Trailer use: hook (best crash/drift frame) -> tease the question ("how does it survive this?") -> hold the reveal until near the end.
