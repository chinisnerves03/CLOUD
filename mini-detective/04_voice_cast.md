## Writing for the voices
- One line per speaker per call. Stitch the audio in your editor.
- Put a tag right before the words it colors; change tags only where the delivery changes.
- Punctuation drives delivery: commas for beats, an ellipsis (…) for a longer pause, em-dash (—) for a short cut-off, a CAPITALIZED word for emphasis. Use capitals sparingly.
- Numbers and names stay as written. If a voice mispronounces a name, add a respelling or IPA only for that word and record it in the table below.
- Generate 2 takes per line and pick one. Do not regenerate repeatedly; each generation uses credits.

## Sound design palette (add in your editor, free library)
- Paper tear: clue card reveal
- Soft three-note tick: the clock losing time (series motif)
- Thread twang: the thief's string tightening
- Wool squelch: Sock Marsh footsteps
- Brass hum: speaking tubes
- Room tone: quiet dusty room, always under the narration
- Music: sparse pizzicato strings and a muted piano, 70–80 BPM, low volume under narration. Use royalty-free or your own licensed music only.

## Audio production order
1. Narrator lines first (they set the timing).
2. Character lines second.
3. Assemble to 30–45 seconds, trim silence between lines to ~0.2 s.
4. Add room tone, SFX, music. Normalize to about -14 LUFS for social platforms.
