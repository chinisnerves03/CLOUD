# Series Bible — Pip Thimble, Tiny Detective

## 1. Logline
In a hidden city built from everything humans have ever lost, a thumb-sized detective investigates why lost things are being stolen back, and slowly realizes the case is far bigger than the city itself.

## 2. Why this works as short-form
- **One case, one clue, one hook per episode.** Viewers get a complete beat in 40 seconds and a reason to follow for the next.
- **Recurring entry line** ("Lost and Found Log, entry N") creates a ritual and a number to count toward.
- **Small stakes, big mystery.** Each episode is a cozy puzzle, while the season arc keeps escalating.
- **No face on camera.** Characters are cut-out puppets; production is repeatable and fast.
- **Universal theme.** Everyone has lost something. Comments will fill with "I lost my…" which feeds engagement and future ideas.

## 3. Tone
Cozy mystery with a shadow behind it. Think late-evening storytelling: dry humor, warm details, and an unease that grows. The narrator never panics. The camera lingers on tiny details.

## 4. Episode template (30–45 s)
| Time | Beat | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 s | HOOK | Log line + one strange fact or image |
| 3–12 s | SETUP | Where we are, what is wrong |
| 12–28 s | INVESTIGATION | Pip looks, a short joke, one clue is shown big on screen |
| 28–38 s | TURN | The clue means something unexpected |
| 38–45 s | CLIFFHANGER + END CARD | Open question. "Entry N+1 tomorrow." |

## 5. Signature elements (keep these every episode)
- Opening: "Lost and Found Log, entry [N]."
- Pip's line when he finds the clue: "Small clue. Big picture."
- The clue appears as a large close-up card with a paper-tear sound.
- The keyhole-eye symbol appears at least once per episode, even if only in the background.
- A single repeated sound motif: a soft three-note tick (the clock losing time).
- End card: cream paper, ink-navy text, small keyhole eye.

## 6. Running gags
- Juno: "Nobody asked, but…" followed by a correct deduction Pip missed.
- Fuzz: every rumor has a price in crumbs, and the price keeps rising.
- Brass: "Case closed!" said while the case is clearly open.
- Pip's thimble hat gets bonked, polished, or dented at least once per three episodes.
- Cog's clock is always 11 minutes wrong.

## 7. What we never do
- No real brands, no existing franchise characters, no copied designs.
- No gore, no real-world violence. Danger is physical comedy and mystery.
- No long exposition. If a line does not move the case or the mood, cut it.
- No ending without a hook.

## 8. Season structure
| Season | Entries | Arc |
|---|---|---|
| 1 "The Keyhole Eye" | 1–10 | Thefts, the Society, the Collector, the giant Eye |
| 2 "The Giant's Lost Thing" | 11–30 | What the Giant wants, Pip's origin, the Drift's source |
| 3 "Above the Ceiling" | 31–50 | Humans and Thimblefolk meet |

Each season ends on a reveal that changes the scale of the world.

## 9. Posting plan (first 14 days)
- Days 1–10: one entry per day (Entries 1–10).
- Days 11–14: "Case file" recaps, behind-the-scenes of the paper cut-out process, and Entry 11–12 teasers.
- Always post at a consistent time. Pin Entry 1 on the profile.
- Caption formula: question about the cliffhanger + "Entry N tomorrow." Comment-bait line: "What did YOU lose that never came back?"
